# Candidates — Fridge Roast: One Line, Biggest Laugh

Judge rubric, most important first: (1) roast aimed at the fridge itself / app /
firmware / ice maker; (2) punchline lands on first read; (3) specific to smart
fridges; (4) tight wording; (5) dodges the obvious cracks (fridge ordering
groceries, fridge watching you, firmware update at 3 a.m.).

## The 8 candidates

1. "My fridge's touchscreen froze, so now I own a $4,000 whiteboard that judges my leftovers."
2. "My smart fridge pushed a firmware update at 3 a.m. and now it won't open until I rate the experience."
3. "The ice maker has one job and a Wi-Fi password, and it still dispenses nothing but notifications."
4. "My fridge knew the milk expired three days ago and still texts me 'we need to talk' every morning."
5. "I paid extra for a fridge camera so I could see my eggs from work; all it shows is my own bad decisions."
6. "My fridge's app has 47 settings and not one of them makes ice — it's a $3,000 push-notification machine."
7. **"My smart fridge's touchscreen lags longer than the milk lasts — it freezes, just never the food."** ← WINNER
8. "My smart fridge's idea of energy saving is dimming its screen while its ice maker screams like a dying printer."

## Pairwise duels (winner in bold)

- 7 vs 1: **7** — 1's "whiteboard that judges leftovers" is good, but 7's double
  meaning of *freezes* (screen locks up / food doesn't stay cold) is a sharper
  turn with fewer words.
- 7 vs 2: **7** — 2 is a firmware-update gag, one of the obvious cracks the
  judge explicitly discounts (criterion 5).
- 7 vs 3: **7** — 3 leans on the "chose violence / one job" meme template;
  7's wordplay is fresher and tighter.
- 7 vs 4: **7** — 4's "we need to talk" text is a decent turn, but
  expired-milk nagging is a well-worn smart-fridge angle.
- 7 vs 5: **7** — 5 is really a roast of the owner, not the fridge, so it
  loses on criterion 1.
- 7 vs 6: **7** — 6 is the closest challenger ("$3,000 push-notification
  machine" is a strong closer), but its 47-settings setup is longer and
  7's punchline lands in fewer words.
- 7 vs 8: **7** — 8's "screams like a dying printer" is vivid, but the joke
  sprawls across two clauses; 7 is one clean comparison plus the turn.

## Winner rationale

#7 wins every duel. It is a roast of the fridge itself (its touchscreen, the
very thing that makes it "smart"), it is specific to smart fridges (a dumb
fridge has no touchscreen to lag), it is tight (one comparison, one turn), and
it dodges the obvious cracks. The punchline — *it freezes, just never the
food* — flips the word "freezes" against the fridge's one actual job, so the
laugh lands on the first read without explaining itself.

Winning line (96 characters, within the 1–200 gate):

> My smart fridge's touchscreen lags longer than the milk lasts — it freezes, just never the food.
